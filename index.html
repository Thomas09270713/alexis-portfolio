Alexis Symone Thomas

I've sat in every seat a building has.
Now I sit in yours.

Founder, Maison Symone Advisory Group · Miami

Real estate clicked for me almost immediately. I walked into my first job as a leasing agent with no real estate or sales experience, at a brand-new community in the middle of its lease-up. Within a few months it was leased, I was promoted to property manager, and I understood how wealth is built in real estate. I knew this was where I belonged.

Since then I've worked every seat that produces an owner's numbers: the leasing desk, the property office, the budget meeting and the investment committee. Most owners get to know their property through a monthly report. I know what happens before the report is written.

01 · The leasing desk

That first lease-up taught me the most honest lesson in real estate: every number an owner reads begins with a person deciding to stay or leave. I learned to tour, close and renew, then to run the property itself, across two new communities in Connecticut. It's the least glamorous seat in the business, and I still think like someone who sat in it.

02 · The buildings

Next came nine buildings in New Haven: 450 apartments, 350,000 square feet of office and a $13 million capital plan. Roofs, HVAC, amenities, and a $750,000 elevator modernization in a historic building. I learned that capital is a promise to future tenants, and that the owner pays for every promise that runs late or over budget.

03 · The turnaround

Then I was sent where things were going wrong. One community sat at 75% occupied. Within six months it was at 99%. There was no single trick: just pricing, leasing, tighter operations and an on-site team working the plan every day.

04 · Every line of the budget

I wanted to know the parts of real estate a rent roll never shows. I took on 2.2 million square feet of office, medical office and retail across 13 properties, with an 11-person leadership team. A $22 million Class A repositioning was followed by 15% NOI growth, and line-by-line work on expenses, CAM reconciliations and contracts returned $500,000 a year to ownership. Savings like that rarely come from one decision. They come from reading every line.

05 · Writing the standard

Then I moved inside a $142 billion investment platform as an investment governance and operations analyst. I created the policies and procedures that every third-party property manager on its owned assets was held to. I onboarded and off-boarded more than 150 property management firms against that standard, set up acquisitions and dispositions, and served as secretary to the investment committee. I didn't just learn the institutional standard. I wrote it.

06 · The owner's seat

Most recently I've held the seat that matters most: ownership. Twelve communities across four Florida markets, more than 4,100 apartments and over $1 billion in value, where every decision lands in an investor's distribution. One community came to us at 89% occupied. Three months later it was projected at 97%.

I know where the numbers come from. So I know where they go wrong.
